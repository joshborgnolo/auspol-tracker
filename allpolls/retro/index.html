About these polls

One row per poll

Every row is one published poll, never modelled or filled in. Each pollster’s name links to its own release, or to the report its figures were read from. A poll marked † is kept here but doesn’t count towards the tracker’s figures, such as a pooled release that re-counts polls already in, a Tasmanian-only poll or an SMS poll. Point at the mark for the reason.

Two-party figures

The implied figure runs a poll’s primary votes through one fixed set of preference flows, the same for every pollster. Against the Coalition the flows are the ones the AEC counted at the 2025 election. No election has ever counted a Labor v One Nation finish, so that contest runs on the site’s own estimated flows. The published figure is the pollster’s own, where it printed one.

Month rows

Sorted by date, the polls run in month groups: the month they were released, or the month they were fielded when sorted by fieldwork. On the two-party and primary tabs each month row also gives the tracker’s own figures for that month: the two-party average on each basis, or the primary vote.

Lean and house lean

Lean is how far a poll’s implied figure sits from the average of the other polls fielded within three weeks of it. House lean is how far its pollster has usually sat from the others, as of that poll. The bar draws both: the square is the poll, the line through it the poll’s 95% margin of error, and the tick its pollster’s usual lean. When the line misses the tick, the poll sits further from where its pollster usually lands than its margin of error, as about one poll in 20 should.

Leadership

The tab lists the polls that asked about the leaders. Net ratings are approval minus disapproval. Some pollsters ask about favourability instead, favourable minus unfavourable; an asterisk marks those. Better PM gives Albanese’s share, then his opponent’s.

Direction and issues

Direction is the share saying the country is heading in the right direction or is on the wrong track. Issues shows, for the six issues polls ask about most, the party the most voters rate best and its lead over the next. Two pollsters ask no voting question, so their polls appear only on these tabs: SEC Newgate on both, Ipsos on issues.

Groups

Demographics compares two of a poll’s own groups, such as 18–34 against 55+. Each figure is a party’s share in the first group less its share in the second. Pollsters draw their groups differently, so each row names the pair it used. Offcuts holds the cuts no other split can, such as birth country or religion, each family in a table of its own.

Economic sentiment

Confidence lists the economic gauges release by release: the consumer gauges of ANZ–Roy Morgan, Westpac–MI and DemosAU, the business ones of Roy Morgan and NAB. They aren’t polls of voting intention and never count as polls. Most are indexes where 100 is neutral; NAB prints a net balance, where 0 is neutral.

Best efforts are made to make the aggregate figures transparent, trustworthy, statistically sound, and informative, but they are, in the end, estimates only.
