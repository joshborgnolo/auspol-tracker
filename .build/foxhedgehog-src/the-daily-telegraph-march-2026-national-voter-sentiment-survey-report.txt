RESEARCH METHODOLOGY STATEMENT
Research conducted by: Fox & Hedgehog
Client commissioning research: Self-initiated
Fieldwork dates: 24-25 March 2026
Mode of data collection: Recruited from online research panels
Target population: Eligible Australian voters (18+, Australian Citizens)
Sample Size: 1810
Effective sample size post-weighting: 1041
Margin of error: 3.0% (larger for subgroups)
Weighting variables and method: RIM Weighting. Age x Gender x Education, Region, Income, State x 2025 Past Vote (Federal)
Position of voting intention questions: After demographics
3PP & TPP preference distribution method: Respondent allocated
   Three-party preferred (3PP): Respondents who selected Greens, Independent or Other as their first preference were asked which of Labor, the Liberal /
   Nationals or One Nation they would prefer in a three-way contest. These responses were then allocated to Labor, the Liberal / Nationals or One Nation and
   added to their respective primary votes to produce the 3PP result.
   Two-party preferred (2PP) match-ups: Separate questions were asked for each hypothetical contest; Labor vs Liberal / Nationals, Labor vs One Nation, and
   the Liberal / Nationals vs One Nation. For each match-up, respondents whose first preference was not one of the two parties were allocated according to their
   stated preference.
Handling of undecided voters: Voters were provided the option to select “don’t know”. Voters who selected “don’t know” were given a leaner question, with the
same options provided. Voters who expressed “don’t know” to both questions (comprising 3% of respondents) were excluded from voter intention related figures,
but included for other questions.
Australian Polling Council Compliant: Yes
Long Methodology Statement: Available at: foxhedgehog.com.au/news-den

WHO IS STOCKPILING FUEL?
The respondents most likely to state they are stockpiling fuel are young men with high levels of tertiary education.
Older Australians, and Australian’s who have not graduated university are significantly less likely to stockpile fuel.
Despite fuel shortages in rural and regional areas, the survey suggests more people are stockpiling fuel in cities.
