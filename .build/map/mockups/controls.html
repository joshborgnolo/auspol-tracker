Map controls: three redesigns

Today's panel stacks five rows with five black tags: Base, State, Seat type, Jump to, Draw. They carry the same weight, but they do three different jobs. Base and Draw choose what the map shows, State and Seat type choose which seats, and Jump to only moves the camera. Each design below sorts the controls by job and drops the black tags. Each is shown at the panel's 1440 width (860px) and its 1024 width (600px).

A · Label column

Keeps every choice one click away, as today. The labels become a quiet grey column, so the choices line up in one edge instead of zig-zagging after tags of different widths. Base and Draw share the first row, and a rule separates them from the filters. Jump to becomes a "Go to" menu beside the search, since both move the camera. City names becomes a small switch at the end of the Draw row: it's a drawing setting, not a destination.

B · One sentence

The tightest option: the whole panel reads as a caption for the map, with every setting a menu inside the sentence. It saves about 90px of height above the map. The cost is that state and seat-type choices are hidden in menus, so they take two clicks instead of one, and a filter that's on is less obvious at a glance.

C · Two blocks

This design names the two jobs outright. "On the map" holds Base and Draw, and "Which seats" holds the filters, side by side at full width and stacked at 1024. Draw becomes a segmented control because it's a mode switch. The camera tools (search, Go to, City names) share a footer line. It's the clearest structure, but the boxed control is a new idiom on the site, and it's the tallest of the three at 600px.
