ABOUT THIS
RESEARCH
About the MRP Methodology
DemosAU surveyed 6,928 respondents nationwide between 5 October-11 November 2025. This
data was then analysed using a Multilevel Regression and Poststratification (MRP) model of
federal voting intention. The MRP model generated primary vote share estimates for all 150 House
of Representative electorates.

The poststratification frame for the MRP model used Age, Gender, Education, Income, State,
Region and 2025 Federal Election Vote as the relevant demographic information.

Two-Candidate Preferred results for each seat were estimated based on relevant preference
flows at the 2025 Federal Elections.


Seat Safety Classification
The electorate vote shares indicated by the DemosAU MRP should be considered with a
confidence interval at 95% probability of +/- 5%. To reflect this uncertainty, each seat is classified
based on the strength of the result in the following three categories;

   In Doubt - Any seat where the winning TCP is 50-51%, or a trailing candidate can win with an
   increase in their vote of between 0-2%.
   Lean - Any seat where the winning TCP is between 52-54%, or a trailing candidate can win with
   an increase in their vote of between 2-4%.
   Firm - All seats not classified as Lean or In Doubt.


Electorate Classification
For the purposes of this research, electorates have been classified as either Inner Metro, Outer
Metro or Rural/Region. Please see P32 of this report for more information on the classification of
individual electorates.

Limitations Of This Research
Changes to Incumbency
The DemosAU MRP does not take into account any possible changes in incumbency status in any
seats for the next election, given that it may be over 2 years away.

Therefore the estimates for each electorate should be considered on the basis of the current
incumbent MP recontesting.

Determination of final two in each seats
The determination of which two parties/candidates make the final count in each electorate in the
DemosAU model is based on the preference distribution data provided by the AEC for each seat.
Whilst this data is the best available, this data is not static between elections and can change
depending on the party composition of the eliminated candidates.

The confidence interval for vote share estimate is also a source of uncertainty in determining the
final two parties/candidates in each seat. This is recognised in the seat safety classifications. In a
seat where a party/candidate misses the final count by less than 2% and if such a
party/candidate could win a final count, then that seat will be classified as “in doubt” even if the
estimated winning TCP was 52% or more.

TCP Preference Flows
In determining TCP results for a given electorate, relevant preference flows are used from the 2025
federal election for that given seat. If the TCP candidate pairing in the DemosAU MRP model is
different to the election, then national average preference flows from contests with the relevant
pairing at the 2025 federal election are used.

In terms of Coalition-One Nation TCP contests, the preference flows from the Maranoa electorate
are used. For Labor-One Nation TCP contests, the preference flows from the Hunter electorate are
used. While these electorates probably reflect the seats elsewhere where these TCP contests may
occur, given the small number of observations for this data, this adds an extra factor of
uncertainty for the results in such seats.

SUMMARY

Labor in strong position, as rising One Nation vote threatens Coalition
The DemosAU Federal Voting Intention MRP indicates that if a federal election was held in recent
weeks it would result in a more fractured primary vote outcome compared to the 2025 election
result with both major parties losing primary votes; Labor 33% (down 1.6%) and the Coalition 24%
(down 7.6%) .

The major recipient of the lost vote is One Nation which has risen 10.6% to 17%. The Greens are up
marginally to 13% and Others have fallen slightly to 13% (down 2.0%).

On a two party preferred basis Labor leads the Coalition 56-44%, a swing to Labor of 0.8%.

In terms of seats, Labor increases its seat tally to 98 seats based on the MRP central forecast with
marginal gains in Victoria and Queensland.

The MRP results reflect the major trend in publicly available federal polling, a large swing from the
Coalition to One Nation. These results give shape to the rising One Nation challenge which is
largely in Coalition held seats in rural, regional and outer metropolitan Australia.

The MRP central forecast gives a seat tally of 29 for the Coalition (21 Liberals, 8 Nationals) and 12
for One Nation. However, as outlined across the following pages there is a significant degree of
fluidity in these results.

The next Federal election is likely more than 2 years away. This MRP model is a snapshot of
contemporary voting intentions. Its most valuable insight is the shape of the One Nation electoral
challenge based on current polling.

Please see the Results Overview section on pages 13-15 to view the MRP Central Forecast and
detailed demographic breakdowns.

Individual seat projections are sorted alphabetically on pages 16-23 and by ALP TCP (two
candidate preferred) on pages 24-31.

The nationwide two-party swing of just under 1% to Labor comprises a swing to Labor of 2.6% in
Queensland, 1.6% in Victoria, 0.8% in South Australia and 0.7% in Tasmania. There was little TPP
change in NSW and Western Australia.

A significant factor in the two-party swing, especially in Queensland is the more even split of
primary votes between the Coalition and One Nation which negatively affects the Coalition TPP
through preference leakage.


Focus on rise of One Nation
If the central forecast of the DemosAU MRP was a final election result, One Nation would win 12
seats (8% of House of Representatives) off 17% of the vote. This would be the party’s best seat share
since its 1998 Queensland election result of 11 seats (12% of Legislative Assembly) off 23% of the
vote. It would also be the most significant third party result in the House of Representatives since
Lang Labor in 1934.

While One Nation has been contesting elections since the late 1990s, the seat breakthrough in the
MRP has occurred for several reasons in addition to its significantly improved vote share.

Firstly, the Federal Coalition now preferences One Nation on its how-to-vote cards which it did not
in the late 1990's. The TCP flows from the Coalition to One Nation over Labor now exceed 80% where
the Coalition is eliminated before the final count.

SUMMARY
Focus on rise of One Nation (continued)
Secondly, although Labor places the Coalition above One Nation on its how-to-vote cards, where
Labor is eliminated prior to the final count, it is usually in rural Australia where it has little chance of
winning seats and thus does not have many booth worker resources in these seats. As a result,
Labor’s TCP flows to the Coalition over One Nation are now below 60%.

Thirdly, because the Coalition vote is now at historical lows, One Nation can more easily beat the
Coalition into the final count and ride its preferences to victory.

Finally, a significant Others vote - 13% nationally - beyond the Greens and One Nation, still remains.
Much of this vote still is an anti-major party vote. Where the final 3CP is One Nation and the major
parties, preferences can send One Nation from third into the final count and potentially victory.

Demographic features of the One Nation Vote
The DemosAU MRP indicates that once undecided voters are excluded, approximately 1 in 5
Coalition voters at the 2025 Federal Election are now intending to vote for One Nation.

The key demographic differentials in the shift from the Coalition to One Nation are age, education
and location (metro v region). The shift to One Nation is strongest among over 55’s, non-university
degree holders and rural/regional voters. Among voters with this combination of demographic
factors, the shift among 2025 Coalition voters is 1 in 3.

Seats Where One Nation is Competitive
The demographic features of the shift to One Nation shape the seats which the party now has
become competitive in. These seats are generally in rural/regional and also Outer Metropolitan
areas where there is a relatively low share of university degree holders.

While the central forecast indicates 12 seats for One Nation, this is inside a wide range based on
the safety status of the seat. One Nation is only the firm leader in 3 seats, while is at least a small
chance or better in up to 34 seats, albeit 16 seats within this group (trailing) have a minority
probability of a One Nation victory.

 SUMMARY
Coaltion Seats Under Threat
The Nationals as a party are most at risk from the rise of One Nation. Of the 15 seats which elected
Nationals MPs in 2025, the MRP model central forecast indicates that One Nation leads in 7 seats
and the Nationals retain the lead in 8. Again the model indicates a wide range of close contests
and within this cohort of 15 seats, the Nationals only remain firm holds in 2 of these; Lyne and New
England. If Barnaby Joyce were to reconsider running in New England for One Nation, then this may
make the seat more competitive for One Nation.

One Nation are only firm winners from the Nationals in Capricornia and Flynn.

Another feature is that One Nation is polling strongly enough in some safe National Party seats that
it can possibly win despite Labor HTV direction towards the Nationals over One Nation.

The Liberals are also at risk of losing regional and outer metropolitan seats to One Nation. There
are 7 Liberal seats where One Nation can possibly mount a challenge to the sitting Liberal. In
Wright, One Nation is firmly in the lead. One Nation is slightly ahead in close contests in Canning,
Grey and Groom, while One Nation pushes the Liberals into third in Longman, but is narrowly
behind Labor. One Nation trails close behind in Farrer, Forrest and O’Connor.

The rise in the One Nation vote at the expense of the Coalition also increases preference leakage
against the Coalition in many of their tight contests against Independents.

A striking political outcome of the MRP model is that the Liberal and Nationals leaders and many of
their putative alternatives both have very strong challenges from One Nation or Independents.

The central forecast indicates;

   Leader of the Opposition Sussan Ley has a slender 52-48 lead over One Nation in Farrer
   Nationals Leader David Littleproud’s hold on Maranoa is threatened with a 19% TCP swing to One
   Nation, leaving a fragile 51-49 Nationals lead
   Putative Liberal Leadership challenger Andrew Hastie is pushed into third place in Canning,
   where One Nation holds a 56-44 TCP lead over Labor
   Due to a falling primary and preference leakage from One Nation, Dan Tehan is reduced to a
   very slender lead in Wannon vs Independent, the rounded TCP result is 50-50
   Former Nationals leader Michael McCormack trails One Nation 48-52 in Riverina
   The mooted electorate for the transition of Nationals senator Matt Canavan to the lower house,
   Capricornia, is a firm One Nation win on the current MRP model.

The only bright spots for leading coalition MPs based on the MRP central forecast are Angus Taylor
in Hume, a firm Liberal hold (56-44 v Labor) and Deputy Nationals Leader Kevin Hogan in Page
which is also a firm National hold (59-41 v Labor).

SUMMARY
Labor seats under threat
Finally, while One Nation is mostly a threat to Coalition seats, there are a handful of Labor seats in
regional and outer metropolitan Australia in which One Nation are competitive according to the
DemosAU MRP model.

Hunter and Forde are both in doubt with narrow Labor leads according to the central forecast.
While One Nation trails closely in 5 urban fringe and regional seats; Paterson, Leichhardt, Blair,
Whitlam and Hawke.

Going forward Labor will have to face three electoral fronts; Coalition, Greens and One Nation. In
total the MRP model indicates that One Nation makes into the final count in 26 seats vs Labor.
