About This Research
 DemosAU surveyed 8,424 respondents nationwide between 13 January-3 March 2026. This data was then analysed
 in a Multilevel Regression and Poststratification (MRP) model of federal voting intention. The MRP model generated
 primary vote share estimates for all 150 House of Representative electorates. The poststratification frame for the MRP
 model used Age, Gender, Education, Income, State, Region and 2025 Federal Election Vote as the relevant
 demographic information.

 Two-Candidate Preferred results for each seat were estimated based on relevant preference flows at the 2025
 Federal Elections, except for preferences associated with One Nation where preference flows were modeled on large
 scale respondent allocated preference data taken during above-stated polling period.

 The MRP model results for the NSW seat of Farrer should not be taken as a prediction of the upcoming by-election
 result as by-election dynamics are vastly different to those of a general election.

The electorate vote shares indicated by the DemosAU MRP should be considered with a confidence interval at 95%
probability of +/- 5%. To reflect this uncertainty, each seat is classified based on a calculated probability of victory by the
leading party. the strength of the result in the following three categories;

In Doubt: Less than 70% chance of victory. This usually equates to a winning TCP of 50-51% or a where trailing candidate
outside of the final count can win with an increase in their vote of between 0-2%.
Lean: 70-95% chance of victory. This usually equates to a winning TCP is between 52-54%, or a trailing candidate outside
of the final count can win with an increase in their vote of between 2-4%.
Firm: Greater than 95% chance of victory. All seats not classified as Lean or In Doubt.

A Note on Preferences
    Given the significant rise in the One Nation vote and corresponding fall in the Coalition vote, the composition of
    preference flows from these parties may have changed significantly.

    To model any possible changes in preference flow we have used large-scale respondent allocated preference
    flows. The use of respondent allocated preference has been challenging when used in individual polls of 1000 or
    2000. By using a sample size in excess of 8000 we hope to obtain more reliable data.

    Even at this scale we are only using preference flows informed by respondent data in relation to ALP-ONP, L/NP-
    ONP TCP contest and the ON preferences in ALP-L/NP contests.

    In all other cases our preference flow assumptions are informed by previous election preferences as before.

    The respondent allocated preference were obtained from respondents completely ranking the 5 voting options
    (ALP, L/NP, Grn, ONP, Oth). Based on this data DemosAU has applied the following preference flows

    In ALP-L/NP TCP contests, A nationwide ON preference flow of L/NP 78.5% ALP 21.5% is modelled by increasing the
    Coalition share of ON preferences by 4 percentage points in every electorate.

    In ALP-ONP contests the following preference flows were used;

    Inner Metro: L/NP 60-40 ON, Grn 91-9 ALP, Oth 70-30 ALP
    Outer Metro: L/NP 69-31 ON, Grn 91-9 ALP, Oth 58-42 ALP
    Regional/Rural: L/NP 79-21 ON, Grn 87-13 ALP, Oth 53-47 ALP

    In L/NP-ONP contests the following preference flows were used;

    Inner Metro: ALP 72-28 L/NP, Grn 82-18 L/NP, Oth 77-23 L/NP
    Outer Metro: ALP 68-32 L/NP, Grn 70-30 L/NP, Oth 58-42 L/NP
    Regional/Rural: ALP 64-36 L/NP, Grn 77-23 L/NP, Oth 54-46 L/NP
