Net approval & favourability

Leader net approval and favourability

Across the cycle Albanese's net rating has moved from +24 just after the election to the high-teens negative. Ley's fell to -40 before her replacement; Taylor has reset to roughly zero. Pollsters split between two measures – most publish net approval (approve − disapprove), while a few report net favourability (favourable − unfavourable). The two aren't directly comparable, so each is drawn as its own line: solid for approval, dashed for favourability. Pauline Hanson and Greens leader Larissa Waters are reported in only a handful of polls but provide context for the broader leadership picture.

How this is made

Methodology, sources & a short glossary

Polling is an estimate, not a measurement. This sets out how the numbers here are built, what they can and can’t tell you, and what the jargon means.

How the trend lines are built

Each marker is one published poll. Bold lines are centred moving averages (21 days by default, switchable), which smooth across the house effects of individual firms. Where the centred window runs past the latest poll, the line turns dashed to flag a provisional, trailing-only estimate.

Margin of error

Every estimate carries sampling error. The shaded band on each trend is the 95% margin of error implied by the median sample size in the smoothing window — typically ±2.5–3.1 points for one poll of 1,000–1,500. A lead narrower than the band is, statistically, too close to call.

House effects

Firms differ systematically — some print higher Labor primaries, others higher Coalition. The house-effects table shows each firm’s average lean against the all-poll trend. These are descriptive tendencies, not a verdict on accuracy.

Known limitations

Final-campaign polls have missed before: the 2019 Coalition win and the 2025 Labor landslide both fell outside the published spread. Treat point estimates with humility, especially head-to-heads polled by only a few firms. Roy Morgan’s separate Liberal / LNP / National figures are combined into one L/NP number for comparability.

Glossary

Primary vote
The share of first-preference votes a party receives, before preferences are distributed.
Two-party preferred (2PP)
The result after preferences flow to the two leading contenders — the standard measure of who would form government.
Swing
The change in a party’s vote share since the last election, in percentage points.
House effect
A pollster’s systematic tendency to report higher or lower than the field for a given party.
Margin of error
The 95% confidence range around an estimate due to sampling — wider for smaller samples.
Net approval
Approve minus disapprove. Net favourability (favourable minus unfavourable) is a related but distinct measure, drawn as its own line.
Preferred PM
Which leader respondents would prefer as Prime Minister — a personal-standing measure, not a voting-intention one.

Post-election trajectories

Governing-party vote since election day

Seven trajectories show the change in the governing party's primary vote from election day, normalised to "months since election". Typical Australian governments enjoy a honeymoon – Rudd's 2007 government peaked above +6, Albanese's first term was up three, Morrison received a COVID boost. The current Labor term is an outlier, down nearly six points by month twelve, sitting on a trajectory closer to the late phases of Morrison's second term or Gillard's minority government than to any first-term honeymoon.

Labor-vs-Coalition two-party-preferred, indexed to Labor’s 55.2% election win – the traditional 2PP contest.
The pattern that should worry Labor: First-term Australian governments tend to gain support, not lose it. On primaries, Albanese's first term rose two points (32.6% → 34.6%); his second term has slipped from day one and, at twelve months in, sits roughly where the Morrison government was at month thirty. A word on the 2PP chart: it traces the polls' two-party-preferred, not the results, so each completed cycle's endpoint marks where the final polls landed – which in this record was often well wide of the mark. The 2022–25 Labor line slides toward the high 40s on the polls even though Labor went on to win 55.2%; that gap is the seven-and-a-half-point 2025 miss, the mirror image of the polls' 2019 underestimate of the Coalition, and it is larger on two-party-preferred than on primaries because the house effect compounds with preference flows. Read against that caveat, Labor's current two-party vote has eased only modestly from its 55.2% election mark – and reframed against a rising One Nation rather than the Coalition, it sits a little below that election-winning margin.

About this archive

AusPol Tracker is a continuing archive of published voting-intention polling for Australian federal elections, alongside leadership ratings and historical cycle comparisons. Each datapoint represents a single published poll; trend lines are 21-day centred moving averages that smooth across pollster house-effects. Roy Morgan polls reporting Liberal, LNP and National numbers separately are combined into a single L/NP figure for comparability.

Current-cycle source data: Wikipedia, "Opinion polling for the next Australian federal election", retrieved 24 May 2026. Most recent election baseline: AEC, 3 May 2025. Figures are subject to standard polling margins of error (typically ±2.5–3.1 percentage points at 95% confidence for samples of 1,000–1,500).
