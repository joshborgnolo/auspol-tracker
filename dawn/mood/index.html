National direction questions

Roy Morgan

Generally speaking, do you feel that things in Australia are heading in the right direction or would you say things are seriously heading in the wrong direction?

Right direction and wrong direction, with a third column printed as “Can’t say / no answer”.

Source ↗

Essential

In general, would you say that Australia is heading in the right direction or on the wrong track?

Right direction, wrong track and unsure, each printed. The February–May 2022 reports asked “or is it off on the wrong track?”.

Source ↗

SEC Newgate

Overall, do you think Australia, (state/territory), and your local area are heading in the right direction or the wrong direction? (The Australia item is used.)

Right direction and wrong direction only, summing to 100. Its reports print a “Don’t know” column for other questions but none for this one, so it is treated as a forced choice.

Source ↗

RedBridge/Accent

Would you say that things in Australia are… generally headed in the right direction; generally headed in the wrong direction; not sure.

Right direction, wrong direction and not sure, offered as answers (single select; the first two in random order).

Source ↗

Spectre Strategy

In your view, is Australia heading in the right direction, or the wrong direction at this time?

Right direction, wrong direction and unsure, each printed. The 2025 wording is unverified.

Source ↗

Freshwater

Generally speaking, do you think that Australia is currently heading in the right direction, or the wrong direction?

Right direction and wrong direction; the rest (about 13%) is read as unsure. The third answer’s label is not on file, and the 2023–24 wording is unverified.

Source ↗

Ipsos

Generally speaking, would you say things in this country are heading in the right direction, or are they off on the wrong track?

Right direction and wrong track, summing to 100: Ipsos notes that its totals can leave out “don’t know” or not-stated answers, so no undecided share is printed.

Source ↗

Lowy Institute

Overall, are you satisfied or dissatisfied with the way things are going in Australia today?

Satisfied and dissatisfied. “Don’t know” or “no view” was recorded only when volunteered, never offered, which leaves 5% in each wave.

Source ↗

McKinnon

All things considered, how satisfied are you with the way Australia is heading?

Satisfied and dissatisfied (each aggregating two strengths), a neutral middle answer (“neither satisfied nor dissatisfied”, about 15%) and a separate “don’t know” (about 1%).

Source ↗

Trust in government questions

ANUpoll

How much confidence people have in the Federal Government in Canberra, asked beside the public service and their own state or territory government.

Its reports print the share with “a great deal” or “quite a lot” of confidence, the share counted here; they don’t list the other answers or say how non-answers are counted.

Source ↗

Essential

How much people trust the national government, asked beside corporations and family.

A lot, quite a bit, not that much, none at all; the first two count. No unsure share is printed: the four sum to 100.

Source ↗

Essential (federal parliament)

How much people trust the federal parliament, in a list of institutions.

A lot, some, little, none at all, and unsure, each printed; a lot or some counts, with the unsure (6% in December 2023) kept in the base.

Source ↗

McKinnon Index

On a scale of 0-10 where 0 means not at all and 10 means completely, how much do you trust the Federal Government?

0 to 10; 6 to 10 count (“high or moderately high”), 5 is neutral. “Don’t know” is a separate answer, kept in the base.

Source ↗

Scanlon Institute

How often do you think the government in Canberra can be trusted to do the right thing for the Australian people?

Almost always, most of the time, only some of the time, almost never; the first two count. The few who don’t answer (under 1%) are left out of the base.

Source ↗

Australian Election Study

In general, do you feel that the people in government are too often interested in looking after themselves, or do you feel that they can be trusted to do the right thing nearly all the time?

Usually look after themselves, sometimes look after themselves, sometimes can be trusted, usually can be trusted; the last two count. Don Aitkin’s 1969 and 1979 surveys, before the AES, offered only the two ends. The report doesn’t say how non-answers are counted. The 1999 dot is the Constitutional Referendum Study, which asked the same question after the republic referendum (37%, from Murray Goot’s tables).

Source ↗

OECD Trust Survey

On a scale of 0 to 10, how much do you trust the national government?

0 to 10; 6 to 10 count, the OECD’s “high or moderately high” trust. How it counts don’t-knows is unverified.

Source ↗

Edelman Trust Barometer

Below is a list of institutions. For each one, please indicate how much you trust that institution to do what is right, using a nine-point scale where one means that you “do not trust them at all” and nine means that you “trust them a great deal.” (Government in general)

1 to 9; 6 to 9 count (its “top 4 box”), 5 is neutral. The reports don’t say how don’t-knows are counted.

Source ↗

AustraliaSCAN

How much confidence do you have in each of the following? Would you say you had a great deal of confidence, some confidence or little or no confidence in the federal government?

A great deal, some, little or none; the first two count. Murray Goot’s tables don’t say how non-answers were counted.
