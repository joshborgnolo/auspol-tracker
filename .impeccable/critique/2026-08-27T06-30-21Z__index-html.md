## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored for this product, not a reskinned template. The 2PP trend chart's event markers are pinned to real, current happenings ("Bondi shooting," "ONP wins Farrer," "2026 Budget"); the site ships a dedicated ALP-v-One-Nation matchup because that's the genuine, unusual dynamic in this electoral moment; `#root` ships a real static pre-hydration summary specifically so the page still says something with no JS, matching the "works offline" principle; "Past cycles" computes an original stat ("15 months in, Labor sits 17 below the average government at this point") no template chart library produces. A generic polling aggregator could not reuse this unchanged.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json index.html` -> exit 2, 4 findings (2x `bounce-easing`, 2x `layout-transition`), all warning-severity. The browser overlay found substantially more: 21 anti-patterns grouped into `cramped-padding` (7 card instances missing inset padding against their own border/background), `line-length` (8 instances, 92-190 chars/line - `.table-hint`, `.card-sub`, `.hero-caption`, `.leadership-note`, `.und-read-note`, `.np-foot`), `tiny-text` (11.5px on `.und-read-note`, x2), plus one `cream-palette` and one `repeating-stripes-gradient` hit. The detector caught issues the LLM review missed: neither the pervasive line-length problem nor the tiny-text nor the cramped-padding pattern appear anywhere in Assessment A's findings - real, mechanically-confirmed craft gaps a human pass skimmed past. Note the detector's own caveat: it ran in a degraded, regex-only mode (HTML parser modules unavailable) - no computed contrast was evaluated, so a clean contrast bill of health was never actually checked.

**False positives:** 3 of the 4 CLI findings are very likely false positives - both `bounce-easing` hits and one `layout-transition` hit (`.anb-fill`, the net-approval bar) sit next to code comments explaining the exact reason for the hand-tuned easing/width-based transition, and all three are wrapped in `prefers-reduced-motion` overrides. The `cream-palette` and `repeating-stripes-gradient` overlay hits are almost certainly intentional - the class name `body.editorial` signals a deliberate stylistic register, not unexamined "slop." The fourth CLI finding, `.twk-seg-thumb`'s `transition: left, width`, has no such justification nearby and stands as a real (if minor) finding.

## What's Working

1. **The static pre-hydration fallback.** Before React mounts, `#root` already contains a real headline, primary-vote list, and a properly-captioned poll table - visible to crawlers, link previews, and no-JS readers, a deliberate choice in direct service of the "works offline" principle most sites never bother making.
2. **"Past cycles."** Aligning every government since 2010 to a shared "days since election" clock and computing a live comparative stat is genuine analytical work - the most differentiated screen on the site, not a chart template.
3. **The correction flow.** Per-poll context auto-seeded into the feedback textarea, correct autosizing, a failed submission that never discards what was typed. Small, deliberate craftsmanship.

## Priority Issues

**RETRACTED: "Headline digits double-expose during the 2PP matchup transition."** What was captured mid-screenshot was an intermediate frame of a deliberate rolling-odometer digit animation, used consistently across other numeric readouts on the page - a genuine strength (a consistent motion vocabulary), not a defect. Not counted below.

**[P1] The pinned nav-bar 2PP readout doesn't track the selected matchup**
- **What:** The fixed top-bar "2PP ALP 51.9 | 48.1 L/NP" stays on the L/NP matchup even after the reader switches the panel below to "ALP v ON" (54.2/45.8).
- **Why it matters:** A reader who just toggled the view and glances at the corner for confirmation sees a different pair of numbers with no explanation - on a site whose whole value proposition is numeric trustworthiness.
- **Fix:** Either label the pinned figure explicitly ("Headline 2PP") so it reads as a fixed anchor, or make it track the active matchup.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Caption/hint paragraphs run 92-190 characters per line, site-wide** (detector-caught, human review missed)
- **What:** 8 distinct paragraphs - `.table-hint` (x4), `.card-sub` (x2), `.hero-caption`, `.leadership-note`, `.und-read-note` (x2), `.np-foot` - run far past the ~80-character readable guideline, confirmed mechanically by the overlay scan.
- **Why it matters:** This is a Read-mode product whose entire job is comprehension; line length this long is a direct, well-established readability tax, and it's pervasive rather than a one-off.
- **Fix:** Constrain these caption/hint elements with a `max-width` in `ch` units rather than letting them span the full card width.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

**[P2] The matchup + time-range control row is too dense on narrow viewports**
- **What:** Six small tap targets ("ALP v L/NP - ALP v ON  3M | 6M | 12M | ALL") pack onto one line even at 673px width, with no dedicated stack/wrap rule among the site's existing 8 breakpoints.
- **Why it matters:** This is exactly the control a distracted, one-handed mobile reader reaches for - the highest-traffic scenario for a "quick who's-ahead check" - and it's the least forgiving target on the page.
- **Fix:** Stack the matchup toggle above the time-range row below ~480px with real touch padding per control.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Leader-approval bars have no visible shared zero baseline**
- **What:** Net-approval bars (Albanese -20, Taylor -7, Hanson 0) float as filled segments with only one faint tick hinting at zero, not repeated per row.
- **Why it matters:** A bar chart's value is instant length-comparison; without a shared visible origin a reader can't judge "bad vs. near-zero" without reading the number, defeating the visualization.
- **Fix:** Draw a full-height zero-baseline reference line shared across all three rows.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

## Minor Observations

- Cramped padding, 7 instances (detector-caught): several `.card` variants render children flush against their own border/background with no inset - a recurring structural CSS gap, not a one-off.
- 11.5px body text on `.und-read-note` (detector-caught, x2) - below comfortable reading size; worth a look for persona Sam (accessibility) even though Assessment A didn't select that persona this round.
- The detector ran in degraded regex-only mode (HTML parser modules unavailable) - no computed contrast was evaluated, so "no contrast findings" isn't the same as "contrast checked clean."
- Genuine accessibility signals exist despite no formal standard being adopted: proper ARIA roles, `aria-pressed`/`aria-expanded`, `role="alert"` on form errors, `scope="col"/"row"` in the static fallback table.
- "Export CSV" silently disappears below ~1000px width with no substitute offered.
- Archive table's `2×2PP`/`PPM`/`APRV`/`DIR` tags are unglossed at point of use (only spelled out inside the filter dropdown).
- `.twk-seg-thumb`'s `transition: left, width` (the segmented-control thumb) is a real, minor finding - no adjacent justification or reduced-motion guard, unlike the other three detector hits.

## Questions to Consider

1. The pinned nav 2PP readout is the single most-glanced-at number on the site - should it always mean "the one true headline," or should it mirror whatever matchup the reader currently has open? Right now it quietly does neither consistently.
2. The product's stated principle is "nothing asserted without a way to check it" - does a headline that visibly double-exposes its own digits mid-animation undercut that promise more than any real methodology question would?
3. "Past cycles" produces the most original insight on the site - if that's the most differentiated screen you've built, why is it the second tab rather than where a first-time visitor gets steered?
