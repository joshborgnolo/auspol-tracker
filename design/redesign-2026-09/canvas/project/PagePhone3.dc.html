Where One Nation’s voters came from How 2025 voters say they’d vote now · YouGov and DemosAU, last six weeks

Three in five of One Nation’s new voters backed the Coalition in 2025

More than a third of 2025 Coalition voters now say they’d vote One Nation, nearly three times Labor’s rate. The two parties won similar shares in 2025, so that difference in rates accounts for almost the whole gap.

The issues What matters most, and who is trusted on it · last six weeks

One Nation has drawn level with the major parties on the cost of living

Seven in ten voters rank it in their top three. A third of those naming Labor, the Coalition or One Nation as best on it now pick One Nation, up from a fifth in December. One Nation leads on immigration and crime, Labor on health and climate change, the Coalition only on the economy.

About a third of voters name none of the three on most issues, and half on climate change. Where the Greens are offered, they come first on climate change: 26% at RedBridge, 28% at YouGov.

RedBridge and Ipsos word the importance question differently and disagree most on health: 37% in RedBridge’s latest poll, 20% in Ipsos’s. The grey bars sit midway between the two pollsters’ usual figures.

Undecided Roy Morgan, DemosAU, Essential and Resolve

About one voter in twenty is undecided. A quarter of the rest could still switch.

Neither share has moved significantly since the 2025 election. Undecided voters have mostly run between 5% and 7% in Roy Morgan’s weekly polls; the latest pooled figure, 4.4%, is lower mainly because of a single 1% reading.
