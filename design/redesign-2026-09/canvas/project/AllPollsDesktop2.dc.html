How much the polls disagree The spread between polls, against what sampling error alone would produce

The polls disagree on how the right’s vote splits, not on its size

Polls of One Nation’s vote typically sit 2.3 points from the trend through them, and polls of the Coalition’s 2.1: about half as much again as sampling error alone would put them. Add the two parties together, though, and their combined vote varies only as much as chance allows, as Labor’s does.

Spread is how far each poll typically sits from the trend through the polls around it, with recent polls counting more; weighting by sample size would mute the small polls whose divergence is being measured. Sampling error is what each poll’s own sample predicts, with a design effect of 1.6. Measured across all 163 polls; the table’s filters don’t narrow it. How it’s built

A pollster’s lean is its average gap to the other pollsters polling within four weeks of it, all read through the same preference flows, with recent polls counting most. One with few polls is pulled towards zero until its record builds. Because every poll uses the same flows, a lean comes from a pollster’s primary votes, not from how it allocates preferences. Preferences are still flowing much as they did at the election

The pollsters’ own two-party figures sit where their primary votes and the 2025 flows put them: level against the Coalition, and 0.8 points kinder to Labor against One Nation, well inside the margin of ±1.7. A shift in where voters send their preferences would show here first.
