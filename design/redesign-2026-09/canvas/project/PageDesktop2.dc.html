One Nation’s vote climbs with age

About one in three over-55s back One Nation, against one in six 18–34s. Gen Z is the outlier, at about one in nine.

The age gap has widened as One Nation has grown

Every group has risen roughly in step with all voters, so none has moved relative to the whole. In percentage points, though, the gap between over-55s and 18–34s has grown from about 3 points in July 2025 to about 17 now.

Three in five of One Nation’s new voters backed the Coalition in 2025

More than a third of 2025 Coalition voters now say they’d vote One Nation, nearly three times Labor’s rate. The two parties won similar shares in 2025, so that difference in rates accounts for almost the whole gap.

The issues What voters say matters most, and who they think is best on it · Resolve, Ipsos, RedBridge and YouGov, last six weeks

One Nation has drawn level with the major parties on the cost of living

Seven in ten voters put the cost of living among their three most important issues. A third of those naming Labor, the Coalition or One Nation as best on it now pick One Nation, up from a fifth in December, and none of the three is clearly ahead. One Nation leads on immigration and crime, Labor on health and climate change, and the Coalition only on economic management.

Undecided Roy Morgan, DemosAU, Essential and Resolve · since the 2025 election

About one voter in twenty is undecided. A quarter of the rest could still switch.

Neither share has moved significantly since the 2025 election. Undecided voters have mostly run between 5% and 7% in Roy Morgan’s weekly polls; the latest pooled figure, 4.4%, is lower mainly because of a single 1% reading.
